Unit 3 — Reconstructing Abbreviated or Omitted Proofs

From a detected gap to a minimal, auditable argument

A practice unit on freezing a target, auditing quantifiers and domains, constructing a dependency graph, retrieving the right lemmas, and honestly closing or reporting proof gaps.

1 Learning outcomes

This unit has the stable identifier O017-U03. After completing it, you will be able to:

  1. freeze the claim to be reconstructed without silently adding or removing hypotheses;
  2. expand the quantifiers, domains, and definitions that determine the form of the proof target;
  3. turn an abbreviated passage of a proof into a list of obligations with clear inputs and outputs;
  4. draw a dependency graph connecting the target, lemmas, definitions, and hypotheses;
  5. retrieve a lemma by its mathematical contract, then check every one of its hypotheses in the context you are reading;
  6. bring forward planning from the hypotheses together with backward planning from the conclusion;
  7. write a minimally sufficient reconstruction—complete enough to audit, without turning a single step into a new textbook; and
  8. distinguish gaps that have been closed, gaps conditional on a reference, and gaps that remain unresolved.
NotePrerequisites and scope

Unit 1 introduced reconstruction by separating an argument into definitions, assumptions, claims, justifications, and open tasks. Unit 2 taught how to identify the source and version being read. Unit 3 develops that practice systematically: types and quantifiers are frozen, obligations are organized into a dependency graph, lemmas are retrieved through their contracts, and details are tested through a minimality audit. The D–A–K–J–T labels and the minimum spanning tree case from Unit 1 are not taught again here.

Readers are assumed to know functions, sets, preimages, topological spaces, continuity, and the definition of compactness through open covers. All case excerpts are synthetic material created for this unit.

2 The reconstruction problem: not guessing the author’s intention

Consider the following synthetic excerpt.

Let KK be compact and f:X→Yf:X\to Y continuous. Every open cover of f[K]f[K] pulls back to an open cover of KK. Take a finite subcover; its image gives a finite subcover of f[K]f[K]. Hence f[K]f[K] is compact.

The excerpt points toward a correct theorem, but several of its verbs hide work:

  • what an “open cover of f[K]f[K]” means, and in which space each set is open;
  • why the preimages are open;
  • why the preimages cover KK, rather than necessarily covering all of XX;
  • which finite set of indices compactness produces;
  • why the original sets with the same indices cover f[K]f[K]; and
  • what “its image” means, given that the equation f[f−1[V]]=Vf[f^{-1}[V]]=V does not hold for a general function.

Reconstruction is not an attempt to write a proof that might roughly have been intended. It is the construction of an argument in which every addition can answer two questions: why is this step needed, and what warrants its use? If the second answer is not yet available, the step remains recorded as a gap; it must not be conjured into a new hypothesis.

3 A seven-stage protocol

Use the following protocol whenever a source skips a line or a whole page of proof. The output of each stage becomes the input to the next.

3.1 Stage 1 — Freeze the claim and context

Copy or faithfully paraphrase:

  • the target claim;
  • every applicable hypothesis, including those stated at the start of the section;
  • local definitions and notational conventions;
  • locators for the beginning and end of the gap; and
  • the version of the source being checked.

Separate the visible text from the reader’s interpretation. For example, if the page says “KK is compact” but does not say “KK is nonempty”, do not add nonemptiness just because the proof you have in mind wants to choose a point in KK.

3.2 Stage 2 — Expand the target’s quantifiers, domains, and definitions

Write down the type of every object and expand the definition of the conclusion first. This reveals the form of the witness that must be constructed. To prove compactness, for example, saying “take a cover” is not enough. The reader must take an arbitrary open cover and produce a finite index set that still covers the correct object.

This audit also checks for shifts of domain. If f:X→Yf:X\to Y and V⊆YV\subseteq Y, then f−1[V]⊆Xf^{-1}[V]\subseteq X; the expression f[V]f[V] is not even well-typed unless V⊆XV\subseteq X. Many abbreviated proofs fail not because their central idea is wrong, but because an object moves between spaces without justification.

3.3 Stage 3 — State the proof obligations

Replace words such as clearly, similarly, pull back, or the result follows with a numbered list. A good obligation has:

  • inputs that are already available;
  • an output needed by the next step; and
  • a checkable completion criterion.

“Explain preimages” is too vague. “For every ii, prove that f−1[Vi]f^{-1}[V_i] is open in XX” has a definite output.

3.4 Stage 4 — Construct a dependency graph

Create a node for the target, each obligation, each active hypothesis, and each lemma that might be used. Draw arrows from inputs to the conclusion that depends on them. The graph prevents two mistakes:

  1. using a conclusion before its supporting nodes have been closed; and
  2. proving an interesting result with no path to the target.

Leaf nodes must be hypotheses, definitions, already-proved results, or retrievable references. “A well-known theorem” is not an auditable leaf node.

3.5 Stage 5 — Retrieve lemmas through their contracts

Do not start with a lemma’s name alone. Write down the contract you need:

inputs + hypotheses  ──>  output

Then match candidate lemmas against that contract, word by word. Record the exact statement, domain, all hypotheses, and its locator. Make an instantiation table explaining which object in the current case corresponds to each object in the lemma. A lemma has not been “retrieved” if you have found its name but have not checked one of its hypotheses.

If a small result can be proved directly in one or two lines, a local proof is often more auditable than a vague reference. If the result is substantive, use a precise reference; do not copy its conclusion while dropping its conditions.

3.6 Stage 6 — Plan backward and forward

Backward planning asks: for the conclusion to hold, what object or property must be available immediately beforehand? Forward planning asks: what follows directly from the hypotheses and definitions? A reconstruction is usually found where these two directions meet.

A plan is not a proof. The arrow “compactness of KK ⇒\Rightarrow finite index set” is valid only after its input family has been shown to be an open cover of KK.

3.7 Stage 7 — Write a minimally sufficient reconstruction and audit what remains

Write the proof in a natural reading order. Then perform two tests.

  • Deletion test: if a sentence is removed, does an important arrow in the graph lose its justification? If not, the sentence may be unnecessary.
  • Gap test: does every remaining arrow lead back to a definition, hypothesis, local proof, or lemma with a matching contract?

Record each gap’s status as closed, conditional, unconfirmed, or rejected. “Minimal” does not mean as short as possible. It means that no essential obligation is hidden and no additional theory is brought in without a purpose.

4 A reconstruction worksheet

The following single sheet is usually enough to contain the entire process.

Field Auditable content
Frozen claim the statement, hypotheses, and context that are actually available

This sheet is a logical working record, not the full provenance logbook that is the focus of Unit 4. Nevertheless, a lemma retrieved from a source still needs enough version identity and locator information to be checked.

5 Worked case: the continuous image of a compact set

We now apply all seven stages to the opening excerpt. This case deliberately uses the open-cover definition, rather than sequential criteria, Heine–Borel, or the extreme value theorem. Every shift of quantifier and domain is therefore visible.

5.1 Freezing the statement

Theorem 1 Let XX and YY be topological spaces, let K⊆XK\subseteq X be compact, and let f:X→Yf:X\to Y be continuous. Then the image

f[K]={f(x):x∈K} f[K]=\{f(x):x\in K\}

is compact in YY.

This frozen statement does not assume that KK is nonempty, that ff is injective, that ff is surjective onto YY, or that YY is Hausdorff. A reconstruction using any of these four properties would strengthen the source’s hypotheses without justification.

In this unit, “KK is compact in XX” means: for every family (Ui)i∈I(U_i)_{i\in I} of sets open in XX satisfying K⊆⋃i∈IUiK\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}U_i, there is a finite F⊆IF\subseteq I such that K⊆⋃i∈FUiK\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in F}U_i. This definition is equivalent to compactness of KK in the subspace topology, but that equivalence is not needed in this reconstruction.

5.2 Auditing quantifiers and domains

The conclusion “f[K]f[K] is compact in YY” expands to:

for every index set I and family (Vi)i∈I,[(∀i∈I,Vi is open in Y)∧f[K]⊆⋃i∈IVi]⇒[∃F⊆I,F is finite and f[K]⊆⋃i∈FVi]. \begin{aligned} &\text{for every index set }I\text{ and family }(V_i)_{i\in I},\\ &\left[\bigl(\forall i\in I,\;V_i\text{ is open in }Y\bigr) \land f[K]\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}V_i\right]\\ &\qquad\Longrightarrow \left[\exists F\subseteq I,\;F\text{ is finite and } f[K]\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in F}V_i\right]. \end{aligned}

The object dictionary is as follows.

Symbol Domain or type Role
xx an element of XX, later restricted to KK witness on the domain side
yy an element of YY, later restricted to f[K]f[K] element to be covered
ii an element of an arbitrary index set II identifies a member of the cover
ViV_i an open set in YY member of the original cover
Ui=f−1[Vi]U_i=f^{-1}[V_i] a subset of XX candidate member of the pullback cover
FF a finite subset of II witness for a finite subcover

The quantifier order matters. The family (Vi)(V_i) is first chosen arbitrarily; only then may FF depend on that family. Choosing one FF before the cover is known would prove a much stronger and usually false statement.

5.3 List of obligations

The abbreviated excerpt yields five obligations.

  1. P1 — Openness. For every i∈Ii\in I, prove that Ui=f−1[Vi]U_i=f^{-1}[V_i] is open in XX.
  2. P2 — Backward covering. Prove that K⊆⋃i∈IUiK\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}U_i.
  3. P3 — Applying compactness. From P1 and P2, produce a finite F⊆IF\subseteq I with K⊆⋃i∈FUiK\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in F}U_i.
  4. P4 — Forward covering. Using the same index set FF, prove that f[K]⊆⋃i∈FVif[K]\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in F}V_i.
  5. P5 — Closing the quantifiers. Since the original cover was arbitrary, conclude that every open cover of f[K]f[K] has a finite subcover.

P1 and P2 are different: a family can consist of open sets without covering KK, or cover KK without consisting of open sets. Compactness can be used only after both properties are available.

5.4 Dependency graph

Read the following graph from left to right. Square brackets indicate nodes that must have an independent justification.

[D1: U_i := f^{-1}[V_i]]
[V_i open in Y] + [f continuous] + D1
    └─> P1: U_i open in X ─────────────────────────────────┐
                                                           │
[V_i cover f[K]] + [definition of f[K]] +                  │
[D2: x ∈ U_i ⇔ f(x) ∈ V_i] + D1                            │
    └─> P2: the family U_i covers K ───────────────────────┤
                                                           v
[K compact] + P1 + P2 ──> P3: there is a finite F ⊆ I      │
                                                           v
P3 + [definition of f[K]] + D1 + D2                        │

The graph also shows what is not needed: boundedness of KK, closedness of KK, the Hausdorff property of YY, or existence of a maximum. None has a necessary path to P5.

5.5 Contracts for the results actually needed

This reconstruction requires one definitional clause and two microlemmas. No major theorem needs to be retrieved.

Working ID Contract Basis and how to close it
C1 ff continuous + VV open in YY ⇒f−1[V]\Rightarrow f^{-1}[V] open in XX a clause in the definition of continuity
L1 f[K]⊆⋃i∈IVif[K]\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}V_i ⇒K⊆⋃i∈If−1[Vi]\Rightarrow K\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}f^{-1}[V_i] prove elementwise by taking x∈Kx\in K
L2 K⊆⋃i∈Ff−1[Vi]K\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in F}f^{-1}[V_i] ⇒f[K]⊆⋃i∈FVi\Rightarrow f[K]\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in F}V_i prove elementwise by taking y∈f[K]y\in f[K] and a witness x∈Kx\in K

For C1, the instantiation is V↦ViV\mapsto V_i for every i∈Ii\in I. L1 and L2 require neither injectivity nor surjectivity. Both are deliberately proved locally below, because their elementwise proofs are shorter and clearer than an unlocated reference to “properties of images and preimages”.

The identity

f−1[⋃i∈IVi]=⋃i∈If−1[Vi] f^{-1}\!\left[\bigcup_{i\in I}V_i\right] =\bigcup_{i\in I}f^{-1}[V_i]

can replace the elementwise proof of L1, but it is not mandatory. If that identity is retrieved as a lemma, its contract and the domain Vi⊆YV_i\subseteq Y must still be checked.

5.6 Two-way planning

The backward plan starts from the target:

f[K] compact
< need: every open cover of f[K] has a finite subcover
< need: a finite F that still covers f[K]
< candidate producer of F: compactness of K
< need: an open cover of K with the same index set I

The forward plan starts from the data:

V_i open + f continuous
> f^{-1}[V_i] open

V_i cover f[K] + x ∈ K
> f(x) ∈ some V_i
> x ∈ some f^{-1}[V_i]
> the preimage family covers K

open cover of K + K compact
> there is a finite F

The meeting point of the two plans is the sentence: “the family (f−1[Vi])i∈I(f^{-1}[V_i])_{i\in I} is an open cover of KK.” After that point, compactness produces FF; L2 carries FF back to the target.

5.7 Complete reconstruction

Proof 1. Take an index set II and an arbitrary family (Vi)i∈I(V_i)_{i\in I} of open sets in YY that covers f[K]f[K]:

f[K]⊆⋃i∈IVi. f[K]\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}V_i.

For each i∈Ii\in I, set Ui=f−1[Vi]U_i=f^{-1}[V_i]. Since ff is continuous and ViV_i is open in YY, the set UiU_i is open in XX.

The family (Ui)i∈I(U_i)_{i\in I} covers KK. Indeed, if x∈Kx\in K, then f(x)∈f[K]f(x)\in f[K]. Since (Vi)i∈I(V_i)_{i\in I} covers f[K]f[K], there is an i∈Ii\in I such that f(x)∈Vif(x)\in V_i. By the definition of preimage, x∈f−1[Vi]=Uix\in f^{-1}[V_i]=U_i.

Compactness of KK now applies to this open cover. Thus there is a finite F⊆IF\subseteq I such that

K⊆⋃i∈FUi=⋃i∈Ff−1[Vi]. K\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in F}U_i =\bigcup_{i\in F}f^{-1}[V_i].

The family (Vi)i∈F(V_i)_{i\in F} covers f[K]f[K]. To see this, take y∈f[K]y\in f[K]. By the definition of image, there is an x∈Kx\in K with y=f(x)y=f(x). Since the family indexed by FF covers KK, there is an i∈Fi\in F such that x∈f−1[Vi]x\in f^{-1}[V_i]. Hence y=f(x)∈Viy=f(x)\in V_i.

We have therefore obtained a finite subcover from every arbitrary open cover of f[K]f[K]. Thus f[K]f[K] is compact in YY.

The proof remains valid when K=⌀K=\varnothing. In the final elementwise paragraph, “take y∈f[K]y\in f[K]” occurs inside the proof of a universally quantified implication; there is no yy to choose if f[K]f[K] is empty. We do not need to add a nonemptiness hypothesis.

5.8 Why this reconstruction is minimal but sufficient

Part Arrow closed Consequence of deleting it
the original cover is chosen arbitrarily opens the quantifier in the definition of compactness the proof handles only one particular cover
continuity gives openness of the preimages P1 compactness of KK is applied to a family not yet shown to be open
the argument with x∈Kx\in K P2/L1 the preimages have not been shown to cover KK
the application of compactness P3 there is no finite witness FF
the argument with y∈f[K]y\in f[K] P4/L2 the same indices have not been shown to cover the image
closing “every cover” P5 the compactness conclusion does not yet follow explicitly

Conversely, the proof need not prove the Heine–Borel theorem, replace covers with sequences, or show that f[K]f[K] is closed. Adding all of that lengthens the text without closing a new arrow in the graph.

6 Invalid shortcuts

6.1 A cover of the image is not a cover of the entire codomain or domain

From f[K]⊆⋃i∈IVif[K]\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}V_i it does not follow that Y=⋃i∈IViY=\bigcup_{i\in I}V_i. Consequently, it does not follow that the preimages cover all of XX either.

Take X=Y=ℝX=Y=\mathbb{R}, K={0}K=\{0\}, and let ff be the identity function. The single set V=(−1,1)V=(-1,1) covers f[K]f[K], but VV does not cover YY and f−1[V]=(−1,1)f^{-1}[V]=(-1,1) does not cover XX. The proof needs only the preimages to cover KK; demanding a cover of XX would create a spurious obligation.

6.2 The image of a preimage does not always recover the original set

For a general function, the correct identity is

f[f−1[V]]=V∩f[X], f[f^{-1}[V]]=V\cap f[X],

not f[f−1[V]]=Vf[f^{-1}[V]]=V. If f:ℝ→ℝf:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R} is given by f(x)=0f(x)=0 and V=(−1,1)V=(-1,1), then f[f−1[V]]={0}≠Vf[f^{-1}[V]]=\{0\}\neq V.

In the main reconstruction, we do not need to equate the two sets. We only use the implication x∈f−1[Vi]⇒f(x)∈Vix\in f^{-1}[V_i]\Rightarrow f(x)\in V_i for a witness x∈Kx\in K. This is why the abbreviated phrase “its image gives a subcover” needs to be replaced by the elementwise argument L2.

6.3 Openness does not transfer without checking the space

If ViV_i is open in YY, continuity makes f−1[Vi]f^{-1}[V_i] open in XX; it does not say that ViV_i is open in XX. If the available function is only f|K:K→Yf|_K:K\to Y, its preimages are open in the subspace KK, not automatically in XX. That version of the theorem can still be proved using the definition of subspace compactness, but its contract and domains are different. The reconstruction must follow the version the source actually states.

7 Testing hypotheses with counterexamples

A counterexample does not replace a reconstruction. It tests whether a hypothesis used in the graph really performs the work claimed for it.

7.1 Without compactness of the domain

The inclusion map

j:(0,1)↪ℝ,j(x)=x, j:(0,1)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad j(x)=x,

is continuous, but its image (0,1)(0,1) is not compact. The family

Un=(1/n,1),n≥2, U_n=(1/n,1),\qquad n\geq2,

covers (0,1)(0,1). The empty subfamily clearly does not cover (0,1)(0,1). Every nonempty finite subfamily is contained in a single (1/N,1)(1/N,1) for the largest chosen index NN, and therefore misses, for example, 1/(2N)1/(2N). Hence there is no finite subcover. This example breaks the arrow from P1+P2 to P3: without compactness, the open cover does not produce FF.

7.2 Without continuity

Take

K={0}∪{1/n:n∈ℕ,n≥1}⊆ℝ. K=\{0\}\cup\{1/n:n\in\mathbb{N},\ n\geq1\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}.

The set KK is compact by the open-cover definition. For any cover, choose a member containing 00. Because that member is open, it contains 1/n1/n for all sufficiently large nn; only finitely many points remain, and one member of the original cover can be chosen for each remaining point.

Define g:K→ℝg:K\to\mathbb{R} by g(0)=0g(0)=0 and g(1/n)=ng(1/n)=n. This function is not continuous at 00: the preimage g−1[(−1/2,1/2)]={0}g^{-1}[(-1/2,1/2)]=\{0\} is not open in KK, because every neighborhood of 00 in KK contains 1/n1/n for all sufficiently large nn. Moreover,

g[K]={0,1,2,3,…} g[K]=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}

is not compact. The family Wm=(−1,m)W_m=(-1,m), m≥1m\geq1, covers g[K]g[K], but every finite subfamily misses all sufficiently large integers. Here the failure of continuity breaks P1: preimages of cover members are not guaranteed to be open.

These two examples also prevent a reconstruction from silently discarding a hypothesis merely because the abbreviated proof no longer mentions it.

8 Retrieving lemmas and rejecting hidden hypotheses

A result’s name is often broader than its actual contract. Suppose that, after the main theorem, a source adds:

Since f[K]f[K] is compact, f[K]f[K] is closed in YY.

The candidate result readers commonly recall has the contract:

(Y is Hausdorff)∧(C⊆Y)∧(C is compact)⇒C is closed in Y. \bigl(Y\text{ is Hausdorff}\bigr) \land\bigl(C\subseteq Y\bigr) \land\bigl(C\text{ is compact}\bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad C\text{ is closed in }Y.

This contract can be proved locally. If C=⌀C=\varnothing, the conclusion is immediate. If C≠⌀C\neq\varnothing, take y∈Y\Cy\in Y\setminus C. For every x∈Cx\in C, the Hausdorff property gives disjoint open sets UxU_x and VxV_x with x∈Uxx\in U_x and y∈Vxy\in V_x. The family (Ux)x∈C(U_x)_{x\in C} covers CC; since CC is compact, there are x1,…,xn∈Cx_1,\ldots,x_n\in C such that C⊆Ux1∪⋯∪UxnC\subseteq U_{x_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{x_n}. The set V=Vx1∩⋯∩VxnV=V_{x_1}\cap\cdots\cap V_{x_n} is open, contains yy, and is disjoint from Ux1∪⋯∪UxnU_{x_1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{x_n}, so V⊆Y\CV\subseteq Y\setminus C. Thus every point in Y\CY\setminus C has a neighborhood contained in Y\CY\setminus C; the complement of CC is open and CC is closed.

Its instantiation table must take the following form.

Lemma variable Instantiation Hypothesis status
ambient space YY YY in the main theorem available as a topological space
YY Hausdorff no counterpart in the frozen statement not yet available
set CC f[K]f[K] available
CC compact result of the main reconstruction available
conclusion CC closed f[K]f[K] closed in YY additional target

The contract does not match because the Hausdorff condition is unavailable. This is not merely formal caution; the additional conclusion is genuinely false in general.

Take the Sierpiński space Y={0,1}Y=\{0,1\} with topology {⌀,{1},Y}\{\varnothing,\{1\},Y\}. Let X=K={*}X=K=\{*\} have the one-point topology, and let f:K→Yf:K\to Y be given by f(*)=1f(*)=1. The set KK is compact. The function ff is continuous because the preimages of the three open sets in YY are each either ⌀\varnothing or KK, which are open in KK. Its image {1}\{1\} is also compact, but it is not closed in YY, because its complement {0}\{0\} is not open.

If an earlier page of the source might stipulate that all spaces are Hausdorff, an honest note is not “Hausdorff is surely assumed”, but:

Gap ID Claim Status Basis Action
G-H YY Hausdorff holds in the context unconfirmed absent from the frozen excerpt check the context and source version
G-C f[K]f[K] closed in YY rejected as a standalone claim; conditional on G-H lemma contract and Sierpiński example add the condition only if supported by the source

Lemma retrieval has three possible outcomes:

  1. all hypotheses match, so the node can be closed;
  2. a hypothesis cannot yet be found, so the node remains open; or
  3. a hypothesis contradicts the context or there is a counterexample, so that path is rejected.

Only the first outcome warrants proceeding as though the lemma has been applied.

9 Choosing minimally sufficient detail

The level of detail depends on what is already available to the intended reader, not on the reconstruction writer’s preferences. Use the following decision sequence.

  1. If a step is definitional but shifts a quantifier or domain, write its instantiation. In the main case, C1 needs to be stated because it produces openness in the correct space.
  2. If a step is a microlemma forming a principal bridge, prove it elementwise. L1 and L2 each require just one witness.
  3. If a substantive lemma is already available in the same source, cite the result’s number and check its hypotheses; do not copy its entire proof.
  4. If a substantive lemma is not yet available, retrieve a traceable source or mark the gap. Do not fill the absence with a theorem name without a contract.
  5. If a paragraph supports no path in the graph, move it to a supplementary note or remove it from the core reconstruction.

A minimal reconstruction also preserves the exact strength of the source’s claim. The main theorem proves compactness of f[K]f[K]; it does not prove that f[K]f[K] is closed, bounded, or has a maximum point in every codomain. Those conclusions require additional structure and lemmas.

ImportantReconstruction stopping rule

Stop when every path to the frozen claim has been closed and every other gap has a status. Do not expand the work to prove every neighboring result. Conversely, do not declare completion merely because the central idea looks right if an arrow involving a domain, quantifier, or hypothesis still lacks justification.

10 A short audit exercise

A reader offers the following reconstruction of the main excerpt:

Take a cover (Vi)(V_i) of f[K]f[K]. Since ff is continuous, every f[Vi]f[V_i] is open in YY. These sets cover KK. Compactness of YY gives finitely many indices, so we are done.

A type audit immediately finds three defects before we assess the central idea.

  • Vi⊆YV_i\subseteq Y, whereas the domain of ff is XX; f[Vi]f[V_i] is not well-typed in general. The required object is f−1[Vi]⊆Xf^{-1}[V_i]\subseteq X.
  • The preimage family must be proved to cover KK, not simply declared to do so.
  • The compactness hypothesis belongs to KK, not YY.

After these repairs, a step carrying the finite subcover back to f[K]f[K] is still needed. Finding the first three errors does not, by itself, close P4.

12 Hints and answer guidance

  1. O017-U03-H01. The order is: for every II and every open family (Vi)i∈I(V_i)_{i\in I} covering f[K]f[K], there is a finite F⊆IF\subseteq I that still covers f[K]f[K]. Since FF is a witness for the chosen cover, it comes after the family quantifier.
  2. O017-U03-H02. The minimal path is the original cover →\to open preimages (continuity) and preimages covering KK (L1) →\to finite indices (compactness of KK) →\to the same indices covering f[K]f[K] (L2) →\to the definition of compactness.
  3. O017-U03-H03. Use the constant function f:ℝ→ℝf:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}, f(x)=0f(x)=0, and V=(−1,1)V=(-1,1). The correct identity is f[f−1[V]]=V∩f[X]f[f^{-1}[V]]=V\cap f[X]. P4 uses only x∈f−1[V]⇒f(x)∈Vx\in f^{-1}[V]\Rightarrow f(x)\in V.

13 Unit completion task

Reconstruct the following synthetic excerpt as a proof dossier.

If KK is compact and FF is closed in KK, then FF is compact. Indeed, add the complement of FF to a cover of FF, apply compactness of KK, then discard the complement.

Use the convention that KK is a compact topological space, F⊆KF\subseteq K is closed, and compactness of FF is tested with families of sets open in KK and covering FF.

The dossier must contain:

  1. the frozen claim, quantifier order, and a domain dictionary for KK, FF, the family (Ui)i∈I(U_i)_{i\in I}, the complement K\FK\setminus F, and the finite index set;
  2. a list of obligations replacing “add”, “apply”, and “discard”;
  3. a dependency graph whose leaf nodes all have a justification;
  4. the contract and instantiation of the clause “FF is closed in KK if and only if K\FK\setminus F is open in KK”;
  5. backward and forward plans and their meeting point;
  6. a minimal proof that handles the possibility that the finite subcover of KK does not select K\FK\setminus F;
  7. a deletion test for at least four proof sentences;
  8. a gap register with final statuses; and
  9. one claim boundary: explain why this proof does not by itself show that FF is closed in an ambient space larger than KK.

13.1 Rubric

Score each criterion 0, 1, or 2.

Criterion 0 1 2
Claim and domains hypotheses or spaces change some types or quantifiers remain implicit all objects, quantifiers, and ambient-space topologies are correct
Obligations and graph principal leaps remain hidden a graph exists but has unjustified leaves every abbreviated verb becomes an obligation and every path is closed
Lemma contract the lemma is merely named a contract exists but instantiation is incomplete inputs, hypotheses, outputs, and instantiation are checked

A passing score is at least 10 out of 12, with a score of 2 for Reconstruction and Honesty about gaps and boundaries. Adding a hypothesis that is absent, using compactness before the family has been proved to be an open cover, or declaring a gap closed on the basis of a lemma’s name alone requires revision even if the total score is sufficient.

14 Boundary with B80 and other units

This unit teaches logical reconstruction, not computational skills.
